# PART 7: ADMISSIONS AND CONTROLLED ENROLMENT

This is a fundamental part of the institutional positioning.

Skill Mountain Academy must not look like an unrestricted course marketplace where anyone can register at any time without structure.

The academy should operate a controlled admissions system.

### 7.1 Admission Periods

Administrators must be able to:

- Open admissions.
- Close admissions.
- Set admission opening dates.
- Set admission closing dates.
- Manage admission periods.
- Control enrolment availability.
- Reopen admissions when necessary.

The public website should communicate admission availability elegantly.

For example:

- Admissions Open.
- Applications Currently Closed.
- Next Admission Period Coming Soon.

Do not use aggressive scarcity language.

Do not manufacture urgency.

### 7.2 Programme Capacity Management

Every programme must have an administrator-controlled enrolment capacity.

The available capacity must be determined by the actual instructor resources assigned to the programme.

Example:

If a programme has 1,000 qualified instructors and each instructor can supervise a maximum of 30 students, the theoretical instructional capacity is 30,000 students.

The system should calculate capacity from:

- Number of active, approved instructors assigned to the programme.
- Approved student capacity per instructor.
- Existing active student allocations.
- Available teaching capacity.

However, capacity must not be calculated from instructor headcount alone if those instructors are not yet available or properly assigned.

### 7.3 Administrator-Only Capacity Information

CRITICAL:

Programme capacity figures and internal seat availability must be visible to administrators only.

Do not display the following publicly:

- Total available seats.
- Internal instructor capacity.
- Number of seats remaining.
- Internal enrolment thresholds.
- Capacity calculations.
- Internal programme allocation information.

Students and visitors should only see whether enrolment is open or closed.

Administrators should have a private capacity management interface showing:

- Programme name.
- Total approved instructor capacity.
- Current enrolled students.
- Reserved places, if applicable.
- Available capacity.
- Programme capacity utilization.
- Whether the programme has reached capacity.
- Whether new enrolments should be accepted.

### 7.4 Sold-Out Programme Management

When a programme reaches its approved capacity:

- The system must automatically prevent additional enrolments.
- The administrator should see a clear sold-out status.
- The programme must not accept payments for unavailable places.
- The public website should show an appropriate admissions status.
- The programme must remain visible unless the administrator decides otherwise.

If additional verified instructors are onboarded and approved, the administrator should be able to increase capacity.

All capacity changes must be recorded in an audit log.

### 7.5 Quality-Controlled Admissions

The public website should communicate that the academy maintains controlled enrolment and instructional standards.

However, do not introduce a new application or interview process for students unless one already exists or is explicitly authorized.

The existing student registration and payment workflow must remain operational.

The instructor network is the foundation of the institution.

We are not recruiting people simply because they have an idea of a particular skill.

We are looking for individuals who have genuine expertise, demonstrable practical knowledge and the ability to teach others.

### 8.1 Instructor Qualification Requirements

The academy should prioritize:

- Master's degree holders.
- PhD holders.
- Doctoral researchers and specialists.
- Experienced industry professionals.
- Recognized practitioners.
- Certified specialists.
- Individuals with demonstrable professional achievements.

Advanced academic qualifications are valuable, but practical expertise must also be evaluated.

A candidate without a PhD should not automatically be disqualified if they possess exceptional practical mastery and demonstrable professional experience.

### 8.2 Instructor Recruitment Process

Before being approved to teach, every prospective instructor must undergo a structured verification process.

Recommended stages:

1. Application.
2. Identity verification.
3. Academic and professional credential verification.
4. Portfolio and practical experience review.
5. Technical assessment.
6. Practical teaching demonstration.
7. Interview.
8. Background and reference checks where appropriate.
9. Final approval.

### 8.3 Practical Teaching Assessment

Candidates must demonstrate that they can actually perform the skills they intend to teach.

For example:

A graphic design instructor must demonstrate real design work, understand design principles, navigate relevant software and explain professional design processes.

A cybersecurity instructor must demonstrate appropriate technical competence and practical security knowledge.

A web development instructor must demonstrate actual development capability.

The assessment must be discipline-specific.

### 8.4 Instructor Verification Status

The instructor management system should distinguish:

- Application Received.
- Under Review.
- Assessment Pending.
- Interview Pending.
- Approved.
- Rejected.
- Suspended.

Only approved instructors should be permitted to teach students.

Do not publicly display unverified qualifications.

# PART 12: PASSWORDLESS AUTHENTICATION AND SECURITY

The existing passwordless authentication experience must be preserved.

The desired student experience is:

1. Student completes payment.
2. Backend verifies the successful transaction.
3. The system creates or activates the student account.
4. A secure login link is sent to the student's registered email.
5. Student clicks the link.
6. The student is authenticated and redirected to their dashboard.

Do not introduce a mandatory password creation step if the existing authentication system is designed around passwordless access.

However, passwordless authentication must be properly implemented.

Requirements:

- Secure, single-use authentication tokens.
- Short token expiration periods.
- Server-side token verification.
- Protection against token replay.
- Rate limiting.
- Secure session cookies.
- Session expiration and revocation.
- Protection against account enumeration.
- Email verification.
- Audit logging for important authentication events.

Never expose authentication tokens in public URLs beyond the necessary secure login flow.

The platform must protect student information, academic records, payment records, instructor records and private communications.

Do not claim that the platform is unhackable.

Build security into the architecture and communicate privacy commitments accurately.
